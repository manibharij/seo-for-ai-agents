# Earning angles: what would someone actually point at?

Read this after the backlink audit, when the gap is quantified and the question becomes what to
do about it. The audit tells you that competitors have authority you do not. This file is about
the only legitimate response: having something worth pointing at.

It is the hardest part of the discipline and the part most often skipped, because the
alternatives are faster. Buying links is faster. Mass outreach asking for a link is faster.
Both are either against Google's link spam policies or simply ineffective now, and neither is
available here.

**Everything in this file is advisory.** The agent finds the angle and writes the strategy. A
human does every single action in it. See the `never` list in
[`roles/distribution-strategist.md`](../../../roles/distribution-strategist.md), which has no
approval path and an empty `fenceable` list.

---

## First, establish whether authority is the constraint

Before recommending any of this, answer the prior question honestly, because the usual error is
assuming the answer.

Compare your page against the pages that outrank it, on intent match and information gain
(`seo-substance-quality/references/information-gain.md`). Then:

- **Your pages are weaker.** Authority is not the constraint. Substance is, and a link campaign
  will not fix it. Say so, and hand it to the Content Editor.
- **Your pages are as good or better, and you still lose.** Authority probably is the
  constraint, and this file applies.
- **You are not in the results at all.** Check Access before anything else. An unreachable page
  has no authority problem.

This distinction is worth real money to a client, in both directions. Recommending a digital PR
programme to a site whose actual problem is thin content wastes a quarter. So does recommending
more content to a site whose pages are already better.

## The angle has to exist before the outreach

The mechanism that earns a link is simple and unpopular: somebody found something useful enough
to reference. Asking is how you tell them it exists; it is not how you make it worth
referencing.

So the first output of this work is an honest answer to **"what does this business have that
somebody would genuinely want to cite?"**

In rough order of how well each tends to work:

### Proprietary data
Your own numbers, from your own operations, aggregated and published. Nobody else can publish
them, journalists and bloggers need numbers, and a cited statistic keeps earning links for
years.

Most businesses are sitting on this and have never looked: pricing patterns, failure rates,
seasonal demand, regional differences, before-and-after measurements, survey results from their
own customers. The work is aggregating and anonymising it responsibly, not inventing it.

### A genuinely useful free tool
A calculator, a checker, a template, a converter, something that solves a small problem
completely. Expensive to build, and it accrues links indefinitely because people link to things
they use.

### Real expertise, published plainly
A practitioner saying what they actually think, including what they would not do and where the
consensus is wrong. This earns citation because it is a source rather than a summary, and it
requires a real named person, which makes it Identity work as much as Distribution work.

### Original research or testing
You tested the things and published the results, including the unflattering ones. Costly, and
the most citable thing in most markets.

### Being the clearest explanation of something hard
Real, and harder to assess from the inside than people think. Worth pursuing only where you can
honestly say the existing explanations are poor.

### Where the honest answer is "nothing yet"
Then that is the finding, and the recommendation is to build something worth pointing at rather
than to start asking. A business with nothing citable and an outreach programme will get
ignored, which is the expensive version of being ignored.

## Who to approach, and why they would care

Once something citable exists, the strategy names specific people with specific reasons. Not a
list of domains with authority scores.

For each target: who they are, what they cover, which specific thing of yours is relevant to
something they have actually written or published, and what the business needs to have ready
before contact.

Useful categories, roughly in descending order of hit rate:

- **People who already cited the thing you improved on.** They have demonstrated interest in the
  topic by writing about it.
- **People who cite your competitors' data.** They need numbers on this subject and have shown
  they will use someone's.
- **Industry publications and newsletters** in the actual niche, which are far more likely to
  respond than general press and send better-qualified readers.
- **Partners, suppliers, customers and trade bodies** with existing relationships. The most
  overlooked source, and the easiest yes.
- **Sector-relevant directories and listings** that a real customer might use. Legitimate when
  they are genuine directories. Paid link directories are not, and are refused.
- **Resource pages and curated lists** maintained by a person who cares about the subject.
- **Broken-link reclamation**: pages linking to something dead that your thing now replaces.
  Genuinely useful to the host, which is why it works.
- **Unlinked mentions**: somebody already named the business without linking. The highest hit
  rate available and frequently sitting unexamined in brand-mention data. Where the
  environment has DataForSEO, workflow 12 in
  [`seo-orchestrator/references/data/dataforseo-modules.md`](../../seo-orchestrator/references/data/dataforseo-modules.md)
  finds them by subtraction: everyone who mentions the brand, minus everyone who links to it.

## What this never becomes

The boundary is absolute, and drafting is not a loophole. The role does not write an outreach
email "for the human to send", because a message composed by an agent and sent as a person is
the same undisclosed automation with an extra step.

What it writes is the strategy: the angle, the targets, the reason each would care, and what has
to exist first. The human writes their own words to their own contacts.

Refused outright, at any autonomy rung:

- Buying, renting, exchanging or bartering links, including paid guest posts and paid directory
  placements that pass authority undisclosed.
- Private blog networks, link schemes, and anything whose value depends on the engine not
  noticing.
- Automated or templated outreach at volume, which is both ineffective and a nuisance.
- Posting or commenting anywhere as the business. See
  [`surfaces/reddit-communities.md`](../../../surfaces/reddit-communities.md).
- Creating profiles or accounts, which needs a real identity.
- Fabricating a mention, a partnership, a client or an endorsement.

## How to report this honestly

Three numbers and one sentence, and the sentence is the important part.

The numbers: referring domains against named competitors, the relevance profile rather than
just the count, and the specific pages attracting their links. All dated, all with the data
source named, all `inferred` where they come from a third-party tool, because no backlink index
is complete.

Compare **referring root domains, never total backlinks**. One partner with a sitewide footer
link produces thousands of backlinks from a single domain, and a comparison on the raw count
can tell you the exact opposite of the truth. Workflow 7 in
[`seo-orchestrator/references/data/dataforseo-modules.md`](../../seo-orchestrator/references/data/dataforseo-modules.md)
gives the fields and the trap; workflow 8 lists the links that were lost, which is usually the
best-value item in the plan because the relationship already existed.

The sentence: **how long this takes, and that there is no shortcut that is not a risk.**
Authority is earned over quarters by real work, and a report that implies otherwise is setting
up the client to be disappointed by work that was done correctly. The most honest finding this
reference supports is often: "your pages are good, your gap is authority, and closing it means
publishing something worth citing and then telling people about it, over two or three quarters,
by a person."
