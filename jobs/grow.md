# Grow

This is the job where the pack's bias has to flip. Every other job rewards fixing what is
broken; this one has nothing broken to fix. An agent that arrives here and runs a
technical audit will produce a tidy list of minor Access findings and move the needle
nowhere, because Access was never the constraint.

The constraint on a working site is almost always one of three things: you are not
visible for demand you could hold, your pages are not good enough to beat the ones above
them, or nobody outside your site has any reason to point at it. Those are **Demand**,
**Substance** and **Distribution**. Start by establishing which.

The most common way this job goes wrong is treating every page as equal. A site that
earns has pages that earn, and a confident change to one of those is the only move in
this pack that can lose real money. Protect them first.

## Order of work

### 1. Find out what already works, and ring-fence it
`demand/search-data`, `operations/protect-earning-pages`.

Before anything else, list the pages and queries that currently earn: traffic, leads,
revenue, whichever the business measures. Those pages move to a protected list.

A protected page can still be improved, but a change to its title, `h1`, URL, primary
copy or canonical needs explicit approval and gets logged with a date so the effect can
be measured. This is not timidity. It is the only way to tell an improvement from a
self-inflicted drop three weeks later.

### 2. Establish which of the three is actually the constraint
`demand/gap-analysis`, `substance/competitive-depth`, `distribution/authority-gap`.

Diagnose all three before choosing, because the cheapest mistake here is working on the
one you are most comfortable with:

- **A demand gap.** Real, reachable demand with no page of yours addressing it. The
  cheapest increment there is, when it is genuinely present.
- **A substance gap.** You have the page, it is in striking distance, and it is thinner
  than what sits above it. The second cheapest, and the most reliably measurable.
- **A distribution gap.** Your pages are as good or better and still lose, and the sites
  above you have authority you do not. The slowest and the only one you cannot execute.

Say which it is, with evidence. If it is distribution, say that plainly rather than
substituting content work that will not fix it.

### 3. Take the striking-distance work first
`demand/striking-distance`, `substance/intent-match`, `substance/depth`.

Pages already ranking just below where they would earn are the best-value work on a
working site: the demand is proven, the page exists, the gap is usually specific and
closeable, and the effect is measurable against that page's own history.

Improve them for the reader, not for a score. The question is always "is this now the
better page", never "does this now contain the phrase".

### 4. Consolidate before you create
`substance/content-audit`, `substance/cannibalisation`.

On a mature site, merging two half-answers into one good page beats adding a third, and
removing genuinely dead weight beats keeping it. Run the audit before commissioning
anything new, because new pages into a cannibalised cluster make the cluster worse.

### 5. Extend identity into the territory you want
`identity/positioning`, `identity/entity-graph`.

Topical authority is earned by covering a territory credibly, and credibly means with
real expertise attached. Where the plan needs expertise the business does not have, the
output is a hiring or sourcing note for a human, not generated filler.

### 6. Advise on distribution, execute nothing
`distribution/authority-gap`, `distribution/digital-pr`.

Find the gap, find the angle, write the strategy, hand it over. No outreach, no
placement, no purchase, at any autonomy rung.

## What this job must never do

- Change a protected page's title, `h1`, URL or main copy without explicit approval.
- Generate content at scale to fill a demand gap. That is the `scale` job and its quality
  gate applies.
- Report an improvement by pointing at sitewide traffic. Attribute to the pages that were
  touched, against their own before, over a window long enough to mean something.
- Recommend chasing a query the evidence says is held by sites with authority this
  project cannot reach in the relevant timeframe. Say that instead.
- Bump a date to simulate freshness. If the page genuinely changed, let the date say so.

## Output

A ranked backlog where every item names the constraint it addresses (demand, substance or
distribution), the evidence it rests on, the effort, and what verification would prove it
worked. Protected pages flagged throughout.

The honest version of this job's answer is sometimes "your pages are good, your gap is
authority, and that takes quarters not sprints". Say it. The alternative is billing
somebody for content that was never the constraint.
